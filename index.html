Self-Serve Connect Concept
Unofficial product concept by Ketan Pansare. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Alpaca. Built from Alpaca’s public documentation; every app, account and number on this page is fictional.
Concept · Alpaca Connect & Trading API

Partners ship in a day. Traders say yes with confidence.

Connect grows when two things are easy: a developer getting from idea to a working paper-trading integration, and a trader understanding exactly what they are authorizing. This concept tightens both ends of that funnel without loosening compliance review.

Self-serve partner onboarding

Today registration hands a partner credentials, then waits on email. The concept gives every partner an instant paper sandbox and a visible review tracker, so building starts on day one and live access waits only on compliance.

  • Paper access is automatic. Partners can run the full OAuth flow against paper before anyone reviews them.
  • Live access stays gated by Alpaca Compliance, as the docs require. The tracker shows the stage, the owner and a target date.
  • Monetization disclosure is a checklist item up front, not a surprise at review.

Fixes friction A. Moves the metric that matters most to partners: days from sign-up to first paper trade.

Discovery that answers “is this safe to try?”

A trader choosing an app wants to know three things before connecting: what it does, what it can touch, and whether they can try it with paper money first. Each card now answers all three.

  • Categories that match how traders shop: charting, automation, analytics, values-based investing.
  • Permission pills on every card, colored by risk. Read-only reads calm; order placement reads serious.
  • “Works with paper” filter and a Try in paper button that opens the paper-only consent screen.

Consent in the trader’s words

The OAuth scopes stay exactly as they are under the hood. What changes is what the trader reads: each scope becomes a plain sentence with a risk level, and the screen opens on paper unless the app is approved for live.

One place to see and control every connection

Traders can see which apps hold which access, on which account, and which one owns their single market data stream. Switching the stream is one tap, so the 406 error stops being a support ticket.

  • Account type, permissions and last activity on every row.
  • “Use stream here” moves the data stream between apps on purpose.
  • Revoke asks once, in place, then removes access.

How I’d measure it, and ship it

Every change above serves one outcome: more trading volume that flows through Connect apps. I would baseline each input metric in week one before setting targets, since I don’t have Alpaca’s data.

North star Trading volume placed through Connect apps Weekly, split by paper and live, and by app category.

Compliance review stays human. Nothing here removes a gate; it only makes the gate visible and moves work before it.
